### Why Performance Management Matters

Until now, how each of us is doing has lived in scattered conversations and individual impressions. This system replaces that with something everyone can see and trust: clear goals set in advance, a coach who talks with you every month, and a fair review twice a year. Here is why it matters — at three levels.

#### For the organisation

A small team wins by pointing everyone at the same targets. When each person's goals are written, measurable, and approved before the cycle begins, the whole company's effort adds up instead of scattering. Performance management is how a growing organisation stays fair as it scales — decisions about growth and opportunity get made on evidence, not on who is most visible or most recent in memory. It is also how we protect what makes this place work: high standards, honestly held.

#### For your own growth

This system is designed to work for you, not on you. Your goals are drafted by you, in your words, with targets you helped set — so you always know what success means this cycle, and you are never surprised at review time. The monthly check-in is protected time with your coach: a place to remove blockers, get honest feedback while it is still useful, and course-correct early. And the quarterly review starts with your own Impact Statement — your account, in your words, of what you achieved and how. Your development needs come out of that conversation with real support attached, not as an afterthought.

#### For your peers

Performance is not a solo sport in a small team. When everyone's goals are visible and everyone is held to the same scale, you can rely on the person next to you — handoffs are clean, commitments are kept, and nobody carries a teammate's unfinished work silently. That is also why Communication, Discipline & Documentation, and Collaboration are fixed competencies for every single person here: they are the behaviours that make each of us easier to work with. Holding ourselves to them is something we owe each other, not just the organisation.

#### The framework at a glance

Before each six-month cycle, you set a **maximum of 5 measurable goals and 5 competencies** — each with a one-line commitment — with your coach; the Founder gives final approval, and that becomes your Goal Charter for the cycle.

Every month, you and your coach have a 15-minute check-in. At the end of every quarter, your coach scores you: one score for what you delivered (your goals), one for how you delivered it (your competencies), combined into your Quarterly Score.

Half-Yearly = (Q1 + Q2) ÷ 2, and Annual = (H1 + H2) ÷ 2 — simple averages, nothing weighted. The full performance review and feedback session, with your Impact Statement, happens every half-year; quarters are the scoring checkpoints. The rest of this document explains each piece; Annexure B walks the scoring step by step with a worked example.

### 1. The Performance Architecture

Every person carries two scores, both on a 1–5 scale:

| **Score** | **Weight** | **What it measures** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Score 1 — OKR Attainment** | **60%** | What you delivered against the approved goals in your Goal Charter (maximum 5 goals) |
| **Score 2 — Competency** | **40%** | How you delivered it — assessed orally by your coach at every quarterly scoring against your written competency commitments, one rating out of 5 |

#### The 1–5 rating scale

| **Rating** | **Label** | **Meaning** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **5** | **Exceptional** | Consistently exceeds all goals; role-model behaviours. Rare by definition. |
| **4** | **Exceeds Expectations** | Surpasses most goals; strong behaviours. |
| **3** | **Meets Expectations** | Delivers agreed goals reliably. A good, solid rating — the expected centre of gravity. |
| **2** | **Partially Meets** | Delivers some goals; specific coaching areas are named. |
| **1** | **Does Not Meet** | Significant gaps; a structured improvement plan follows. |

*Please read that middle row again: on a 5-point scale, 3 is a good rating. It means you reliably delivered what was agreed — which is exactly what the organisation asks of you.*

### 2. Goal Setting — Before Each Six-Month Cycle

Goal setting happens once before every six-month cycle (two quarters), in the **Goal_Setting_Library.xlsx** workbook. The Competency Library and OKR Library in it are example templates — a starting menu, not a binding list. Adapt them to your real work and add your own.

| **Step** | **What happens** |
| --- | --- |
| **1** | You draft a maximum of 5 goals in the Goal Setting Template — each with a measurable key result, a baseline, a target, and a data source — along with your 5 competencies for the cycle — each carrying a one-line commitment defining what you will actually do under it (e.g., Communication: ‘weekly written project update to all stakeholders’). Your quarterly scoring and half-yearly review assess you against these commitments. |
| **2** | Your coach reviews first — refining targets, checking measurability, confirming your competency selection — and signs the Coach Review column. |
| **3** | The Founder then reviews and gives final approval. The approved sheet is your Goal Charter for the six-month cycle — the single reference for what success means. |

#### Your 5 competencies: 3 fixed + 2 chosen

| **Fixed for everyone, every cycle** | **Why** |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Communication** | Mandatory for every person — clear written and verbal communication is a universal requirement here, no exceptions. |
| **2. Discipline & Documentation** | Mandatory for every person — trackers current, work documented, commitments traceable. The habit that keeps a small company running without chaos. |
| **3. Collaboration  /  Coaching & Collaboration (for coaches)** | Everyone carries Collaboration. The seven coaches carry Coaching & Collaboration instead — growing their coachees is measured, not assumed. |

Your remaining two competencies are chosen with your coach from the Competency Library, matched to your role and this cycle's goals. For every competency — fixed or chosen — you write a one-line commitment: what you are going to do under Communication, under Collaboration, and under each of the others. Commitments make the competency concrete and reviewable.

### 3. Your Coach

Every person has one named coach — your development partner through the cycle. Your coach reviews your goal draft first, meets you every month, verifies your Impact Statement at each quarter end, and owns one development action for you each cycle. Your coach is your first port of call for feedback, blockers, and growth conversations


Coach allocations are maintained by People & HR and shared in the Coach Guide; you will be told who your coach is when the cycle begins, and any change is communicated to you directly.


### 4. Monthly Check-ins

Once a month, you and your coach have a 15-minute check-in — a two-way conversation, not an evaluation. There are no ratings and no status labels. You walk your Goal Charter together, talk openly about what is moving and what is in the way, and agree the way forward before you part. Your coach records a brief summary and the agreed way forward, which you both sign.


#### How to get the most from your check-in

- Come with your Goal Charter open and know your numbers — the 15 minutes are for discussion, not status recital.

- Raise blockers early and honestly; the check-in exists to remove them while there is still time to act.

- Ask for feedback — and expect it to be specific. Nothing at your quarterly review should be a surprise, and check-ins are where that promise is kept.

- Leave with clarity: before the conversation ends, you should be able to say exactly what happens next and who owes what.

### 5. Quarterly Scoring & the Half-Yearly Review

Scores are given every quarter; the full performance review and feedback session happens every half-year — do not confuse the two. At each quarter end, your coach scores your Charter with you in a short scoring conversation: each goal 0.0–1.0 from its data source → Score 1, plus Score 2 from a brief oral discussion of your competency commitments → your Quarterly Score = (Score 1 × 0.6) + (Score 2 × 0.4), calibrated across coaches. At the end of the cycle (every half-year), you go through the full review, recorded on the Half-Yearly Review Template (Annexure A):

#### Step 1 — Your Impact Statement

You write your own account of the impact you created across the half — with real numbers — and share it 3 days before the review conversation. It covers both sides of your Goal Charter: the results you delivered against your goals, and how you lived the commitments under your competencies. This is the only section you write, and it is the most important one: your performance story, in your words.

#### Step 2 — The Coach Review

Your coach verifies your Impact Statement against the Goal Charter, the check-in records, and the named data sources, then writes three things: the impact you have created (their verified summary), your development needs (the areas to improve, with the training, exposure, or support that will be provided), and the score.

#### Step 3 — The conversation and the score

In a 60-minute conversation — the Founder's full performance and feedback session — you walk through the Impact Statement and review together. Your two quarterly scores are already on record; the Half-Yearly Score is simply their average: (Q1 + Q2) ÷ 2. The conversation itself is for feedback, development needs, and the way forward. After calibration among all coaches, the Founder signs off and you acknowledge the form — with space for your own comment, verbatim.

### 7. Fairness & Governance

- The system is public; your scores are private — known only to you, your coach, and the Founder.

- Review order is fixed: coach first, then the Founder — for goal charters and for scores. Nothing changes silently after your conversation.

- Goals must be measurable with a named data source, and your review is verified against those sources — evidence over impression.

- You always see your review in writing and acknowledge it yourself, with space for your own comment on the record.

- People & HR administers the system, and the framework itself is reviewed and refined after every cycle — your feedback on it is welcome.

### Annexure B — The Scoring Algorithm, Step by Step

*So there is no confusion about how a rating comes to be, here is the exact sequence — followed by a worked example with real numbers.*

| **Step** | **What happens** |
| --- | --- |
| **1** | Goal setting (before each six-month cycle): you set up to 5 measurable goals + 5 competencies with a one-line commitment under each → coach reviews → Founder approves → your Goal Charter is locked for the cycle. |
| **2** | Through the cycle: monthly 15-minute check-ins with your coach. No scoring happens here. |
| **3** | Every quarter end: each goal in your Charter is scored between 0.0 and 1.0 against its target, using the named data source. (Delivered fully = 1.0; delivered 80% of the target ≈ 0.8; and so on.) |
| **4** | Score 1 (OKR Attainment): the average of your goal scores converts to a 1–5 rating → 0.95+ = 5 · 0.80–0.94 = 4 · 0.65–0.79 = 3 · 0.45–0.64 = 2 · below 0.45 = 1. |
| **5** | Score 2 (Competency): in the quarterly scoring conversation, you and your coach discuss your five competencies orally against the commitments you wrote; your coach records one rating out of 5. |
| **6** | Quarterly Score = (Score 1 × 0.6) + (Score 2 × 0.4). |
| **7** | Calibration: all coaches and the Founder align the quarter's ratings for consistency before scores are shared. |
| **8** | Every quarter end: the full performance review & feedback session (Impact Statement → coach review → conversation → Founder sign-off). Half-Yearly Score = (Q1 + Q2) ÷ 2; at year end, Final Annual Score = (H1 + H2) ÷ 2. |

#### A worked example

Suppose your Goal Charter had four goals, and at a quarter end they scored:

| **Goal** | **Target vs. actual** | **Goal score** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contact 100% of inbound leads within 48 hours | Achieved 95% | 0.95 |
| Close ₹20L in revenue | Closed ₹16L | 0.80 |
| Maintain pipeline at 3x of target | Held ~2x on average | 0.65 |
| Publish 4 client case studies | Published 3 | 0.75 |

- Average goal score = (0.95 + 0.80 + 0.65 + 0.75) ÷ 4 = 0.79 → falls in the 0.65–0.79 band → Score 1 = 3.

- In the review conversation, the competency discussion lands at strong-but-not-exceptional → the coach records Score 2 = 4.

- Quarterly Score = (3 × 0.6) + (4 × 0.4) = 1.8 + 1.6 = 3.4.

- If your quarters score 3.4, 3.6, 3.7, 3.9 → H1 = (3.4 + 3.6) ÷ 2 = 3.5 and H2 = (3.7 + 3.9) ÷ 2 = 3.8 → Final Annual = (3.5 + 3.8) ÷ 2 = 3.65.

*Reading it: a 3.4 is a solid quarter — goals largely delivered, strong behaviours. Nobody needs perfect 1.0s across the board; goals set with real ambition will average around 0.7–0.8, and that is exactly what a healthy Charter looks like.*

### 1. The Coach's Role

As a coach you are a development partner, not a task supervisor. Coaching conversations are distinct from day-to-day work direction. Your responsibilities across each six-month cycle:

- Review each coachee's goal draft first — refine targets, check measurability, and confirm the 5 competencies each carry a concrete one-line commitment (what they will actually do under Communication, Collaboration, and the rest) — before it goes to the Founder for final approval.

- Hold a 15-minute check-in with each coachee every month, on the framework in Section 2, and complete the check-in record (Annexure)

- Give feedback in the moment, all cycle long. Nothing at any scoring or review should be a surprise — a surprise at review means the check-ins failed.

- At every quarter end: score the coachee — goals in the Charter → Score 1, competency assessment against commitments → Score 2 → Quarterly Score — and bring your ratings to calibration. At every half-year end: run the full performance review & feedback session (Impact Statement, coach review, development needs), where the Half-Yearly Score = (Q1 + Q2) ÷ 2.

- Own one development action per coachee per cycle — a skill to build, an exposure to arrange, a stretch task — and follow it through.

- Raise a serious or repeating concern with the Founder directly, through conversation. The check-in record is not an escalation channel.

*Your coaching is itself measured: every coach carries Coaching & Collaboration as a fixed competency, assessed on coachee progress, check-in completion, and coachee feedback.*

### 4. The 15-Minute Check-in Framework

Every check-in follows the same four stages — an adaptation of the GROW coaching model — so quality is consistent across all seven coaches:

| **Stage** | **Time** | **What the coach does** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Open** | 2 min | Set an easy tone. "How has the month been for you?" Let them speak first — this is their conversation. |
| **2. Goals** | 5 min | Walk the Goal Charter together: what moved on each goal, what the numbers say. Acknowledge one specific thing done well. |
| **3. Reality** | 4 min | Explore what is in the way. Ask, don't tell: "What's slowing this down? What have you already tried? What would make it easier?" |
| **4. Way Forward** | 4 min | Agree the way forward: specific actions, who does what, and any support you will arrange — captured on the form before parting. |

#### Ground rules

- Listen more than you speak — roughly 70/30 in the coachee's favour.

- Ask questions before offering solutions; the goal is their thinking, not your answers.

- Feedback is specific and behavioural — "the client deck went out with two data errors" rather than "be more careful".

- Recognition works the same way: name the specific thing done well, not a general "good job".

- Complete the form at the end of the conversation, in the room. It takes two minutes: summary of discussion + way forward, signed by both.

- Protect the slot. A skipped check-in tells the coachee their development is optional.

### 5. Quarterly Scoring & the Half-Yearly Review

#### Before the conversation

- Read the coachee's Impact Statement (they share it 3 days prior) and verify each claim against the Goal Charter's data sources and your check-in records — results against goals, and behaviour against the written competency commitments.

- Score each goal 0.0–1.0 in the Goal Charter's column for this quarter, with the coachee's numbers in front of you; the average converts to Score 1 (0.95+ → 5 · 0.80 → 4 · 0.65 → 3 · 0.45 → 2 · below → 1).

- Draft the two written sections: the impact the person has created (your verified summary) and the development needs (areas to improve, with the training, exposure, or support you will arrange).

#### In the conversation (60 minutes)

- Let the coachee walk you through their Impact Statement first — it is their story.

- Assess the competency orally: discuss each of their five competencies against the Competency Library's level descriptions ("what a 5 / 3 / 1 looks like"), then agree where the quarter landed overall — judged against the commitments they wrote at goal setting — and record one rating out of 5 as Score 2. Anchor the oral discussion in specifics from your check-in records — moments, deliverables, behaviours — not general impressions.

- Close on development: the coachee should leave knowing exactly what to grow next and what support is coming.

#### After the conversation

- Bring your ratings to the calibration discussion with the other coaches and the Founder — outliers get challenged so a 4 means the same thing everywhere. Come able to back each rating with specifics.

- After the Founder signs off, share the final form with the coachee for acknowledgment. They may add their own comment, verbatim, on the record.

#### Common rating traps

- Recency bias — rating the last month instead of the quarter. Antidote: re-read the quarter's check-in records before scoring.

- Central tendency — defaulting everyone to 3 to avoid conversations. If a coachee genuinely exceeded, say 4 and defend it; if they fell short, say 2 and coach it.

- Halo/horns — letting one big win or one visible miss colour every judgment. Score the goals on their numbers; discuss the competencies on their own evidence.

- Remember the scale's centre of gravity: 3 is a good rating, and a goal-score average of 0.7–0.8 is what a well-set Charter produces. Scoring happens every quarter; the full review and feedback session happens every half-year.
